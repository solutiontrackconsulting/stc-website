# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Enterprise IT managers and directors at mid-to-large organizations running Cisco UC environments. They are dealing with a concrete, near-term problem: a pending migration, a compliance gap, an inherited infrastructure of unknown quality, or a vendor that can't staff senior engineers. They have technical context (they know what CUCM and E911 mean) but limited time and high stakes. They are not shopping for general consulting; they are looking for the specific person who has solved this exact problem before.

A secondary audience is IT service providers and MSPs who need white-label senior engineering capacity for their own clients' Cisco environments, without adding headcount.

## Product Purpose

Solution Track Consulting is the practice of Stephen Frahm, a veteran with 25+ years of Cisco UC experience in the Pacific Northwest. The site exists to generate consulting engagements: discovery calls, audit commissions, and partner agreements. Success means a qualified prospect books a meeting or sends an inquiry. The site is not a brand-awareness play; every page should move toward that action.

## Brand Personality

Disciplined. Direct. Trustworthy.

The voice comes from a veteran background: no hedging, no upsell, no consulting jargon. Plain declarative sentences. The authority is in the specificity of the work (forensic audits, E911 compliance, CUCM migrations), not in volume of claims. Tone is calm, senior, and confident without being aggressive.

## Anti-references

- Generic IT vendor sites (Cisco, Avaya, NTT): stock photography of server rooms, gradient hero banners, buzzword-heavy feature grids.
- SaaS startup aesthetic: pastel gradients, rounded cards, "Trusted by 10,000+ teams" social proof, hero metrics, excessive whitespace softness.
- Big-4 consulting firms (McKinsey, Deloitte): overly formal, article-heavy, zero personality, institutional distance.
- Freelancer/portfolio sites: too casual, too personal, not serious enough for enterprise procurement.

## Design Principles

1. **Specificity earns trust.** Services are named like forensic disciplines because they are. Vague language undermines the authority the site needs to project. Every claim should be concrete and verifiable.

2. **Name the risk.** Enterprise IT managers come here when something could go wrong. Surface the cost of inaction plainly. Don't soften it to be polite.

3. **Senior presence, not agency scale.** One principal, no junior staff. Copy and design should feel like a direct conversation with that principal, not a firm's capabilities brochure.

4. **Authority without performance.** The credibility is in 25 years of hands-on work, not in metric counters or client logo walls. Don't dress this up like a product company proving growth.

5. **Plain language for technical buyers.** The audience speaks Cisco. Don't explain acronyms. Do explain what the gap costs.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. The audience skews toward experienced enterprise IT professionals; assume a desktop-first reading context while maintaining mobile usability.
